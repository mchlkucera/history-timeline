Timeline · vertical

The zoomable timeline, rotated. Time runs down, past at top; bands become columns.

Identical engine to the horizontal view — same level-of-detail thresholds, same five shapes, same eight domain colours, same lenses and filters. Two things change. A label no longer has to fit inside the time it describes: every mark gets its own full line of text beside it. And the columns are no longer squeezed into the window — each one takes the width its labels actually need, so the whole surface is usually wider than what you can see. Drag it like a map: up and down through time, left and right across the world.

Scroll to zoom time. Drag anywhere to travel — vertically through the years, horizontally across the columns, in the same gesture. The strip under the plot is the whole surface in miniature: the bright frame is the part you can see, and you can drag it too. Columns fade out at an edge when there is more that way; the chip in the corner names the next one — click it to bring it in. Shape = what kind of thing (a moment, a stretch of time, a life, a territory, an era). Color = which domain it belongs to. Click any event to open it on Wikipedia. Faint hairlines mean a label had to step aside to stay legible — the mark is always at the true year.
