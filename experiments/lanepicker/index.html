Local prototype · not deployed · experiments/lanepicker

A card that offers the lanes a thing could live in

“Show in timeline is not enough.” Four arrangements of the same idea — D first, because it is the one to build, then the three it was cut down from — each rendered twice — once for Daoism, which is in no lane at all, and once for Mozart, who plausibly fits several. Every lane name, every count, every neighbour and every connection below is read out of the shipped corpus by build-data.py. Nothing is invented. Where a list would be empty, the card says so instead of filling itself.

The cards are the real component — design/selcard/selcard.css, loaded unmodified — on the real tokens, in both themes. The one new block sits between the identity and the destinations, because “is this thing even here” has to be settled before “show this in” can mean anything.

Why the current model cannot express the question

Six facts, each checked against the files. They are the reason this is a model problem wearing a UI problem’s clothes.

One lane
factsOf(id) computes exactly one canonical lane — layerIdFor(band, category, type), e.g. Europe × power × polity — plus adopted[], the lanes whose hard-coded anchors list names that id. planReveal then takes the cheapest candidate and reveal() acts. The reader is never shown a choice because the model never had one.
Two axes
The derived axis is region × domain (31 layers). data/lanes.json holds a completely different axis: 15 curated lanes with explicit member lists — Arts & movements, Religion, Technology, Czech history. A thing can plausibly belong on both at once. That is exactly “Mozart as part of Austria or Music”, and no field in the model can hold both answers.
Band ≠ place
A mark’s band is either a region or a lane key, never both. Mozart’s band is MU, so a composer who lived in Salzburg and Vienna has no region at all; layerIdFor() never asks his place. And there is no Austria to ask for: the region axis has four bands — EU, ME, AS, AM. The closest thing the derived axis can offer is Europe · Art & culture, which holds seven things: Stonehenge, the first Olympic games, Leonardo, Michelangelo, Shakespeare, Kafka and the Renaissance.
Daoism
eventById('belief:daoism') returns null, so factsOf returns null and planReveal says never. A belief stream has no band, no category, no type and no coordinate — layerIdFor() has nothing to compute from. But lanes.json ships a Religion lane with 40 members. “Daoism cannot be on the timeline” is a limitation of the model, not a fact about Daoism.
Same conversation, two fates
Confucius is drawn — he is a LIVES row banded AS, so he resolves to Asia · Essentials (35 things). Daoism is a beliefs.json stream, so it resolves to nothing. The single link between them, entity:Confucius → belief:daoism, is Daoism’s only connection in a corpus of 348.
What you join
A count alone does not answer “what context am I adding”. Religion’s 40 members run from the Axial Age to the Iranian Revolution, and 36 of them sit west of longitude 62; the easternmost is Buddhist expansion at 85°E. Nothing in it is Chinese. Philosophy’s 35 members are all west of 28°E — it is a lane of European philosophy that is not named that. Adding Daoism to either is a real editorial act, and the card should say so before you click.

Arrangement D — the list of viewsRecommended

“We just have a list of views. […] The moment we have him open in Timeline […] we should see an expanded view to specify which lanes in the Timeline it is a part of. Just show their names, nothing else, no other explanations.”

The destination strip the card already ships is the list of views. The lane list hangs off it, and only under Timeline — press Flow, Map or Cube on the stage switch and it closes, because a lane is a timeline concept. Two states, both borrowed from the strip rather than invented: the inverted ink block is a lane on your board, the plain bordered cell is one you can press to add. Mozart starts with Music already on the board so both states are visible at once; Daoism starts with nothing, which is the case the founder described as a card that is in none of your lanes yet.

Good at
It adds no vocabulary — one existing control grows one row of names — and it is the only arrangement that answers “which have I added” at a glance instead of by reading. Smallest of the four: 315px for Daoism, 422px for Mozart, both inside the card’s 480px cap.
Costs
The order of the names is unexplained, so the ranking has to be right by feel; a reader who does not know a lane cannot find out what is in it from here; and the two states are carried by fill alone, which is one signal doing two jobs.

The three it was cut down from

How D was arrived at, kept because the arguments in them are what settled the default and the order. Same data, same tokens, same card; only the arrangement of the offer differs. Every specimen is live: pick a lane, press the button, read the notice, press Undo. The measurement under each card is its real rendered height against the card’s real 480px cap.

Arrangement A One default, one link
Good at
The common path is one click and the card does not grow — 386px for Daoism, 429px for Mozart, both inside the card’s real 480px cap. The default is stated in the button, so nobody is asked a question they did not come with.
Costs
The choice is discovered, not read — a reader who never presses “others” never learns that lanes are a decision, which is the thing the founder asked for.
Arrangement B The ladder
Good at
Every candidate is read, ranked, with its size, its reason and a glimpse of what is inside — and the top one is pre-selected, so it is still one click.
Costs
Height, and only height. Shown uncapped on purpose: 593px for Daoism’s two candidates, 921px for Mozart’s six, against a 480px cap. It would have to show three and put “All 6 →” in the header.
Arrangement C Two axes
Good at
It splits the choice the way the founder said it — “Austria or Music” — and an empty axis becomes a sentence in the column where the answer should have been, which is the clearest statement of the gap anywhere on this page.
Costs
Two headers and an empty-state paragraph is a lot of chrome for 320px — 762px and 1016px against a 480px cap, the tallest of the three — and it promises a symmetry the data does not have: one axis is empty for Daoism and seven-things-deep for Mozart.

The note

The rule behind the lists above — which still runs D, silently — the default, what I could not settle, and what has to move in layers.ts for any of it to be real.

The candidate-derivation rule

Four sources, in falling order of confidence. Implemented in build-data.py → candidates(); every row on every card above carries the reason it passed.

  1. Where it already is. Any lane whose member list names the id, or whose anchors adopt it. (Mozart: the Mozart lane anchors entity:Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart.)
  2. Where the model computes it. layerIdFor(band, cat, type) — the single lane the app derives today. (Mozart: band MU → Music.)
  3. Where it happened on the ground. The region lane its place would give, via regionOf(lat, lon). This is not computed today and every row that comes from it says so. (Mozart: Salzburg → EU → Europe · Art & culture.)
  4. What its subject fits. Every curated lane, scored: +3 if the lane is single-domain and that domain is the subject’s; +2 if the lane’s span contains the whole subject, +1 if it contains only its start; +1 per shared word, up to three. Kept at 4 or better.

Two gates do most of the work, and both exist so the list has no hand-written exceptions. Purity: a lane where ≥90% of members share one category will not take a foreigner at any word score — that is what keeps Mozart out of Religion. A mixed lane (Czech history: power, art, belief, society) is about a place, not a domain, so it takes whatever happened there but has to earn it on evidence. Origin: a lane whose span begins after the subject does cannot hold its origin — that is what keeps Daoism (−350) out of Political ideologies (1685) without anyone writing that down.

And a lane of my own is always the last row, never scored. It is a sketch: nothing backs it. Its place in the list is the argument — it belongs beside the other answers, not behind a separate “create” affordance, because at the moment you are looking at three lanes that are nearly right, “none of these” is the fourth answer to the same question.

The default, and why

The default is the lane the app would have picked anyway. Concretely: planReveal’s own answer — the owning lane, then an adopting one, cheapest first. For Mozart that is Music, which is exactly what search does with him today.

The defence is that it changes nobody’s behaviour. A reader who does not look at the list gets the result they already get, so the picker is purely additive: it puts a choice where today there is none. It is also the only default already written and already tested — the alternative (“the lane with the most members”, “the lane you used last”) is a new opinion that has to be maintained.

When there is no such lane — planReveal says never, which is every belief stream — the default falls to the top-scoring subject-fit lane. For Daoism that is Religion (6) over Philosophy (5). Here the default is a new opinion, so the card earns it by showing the reason on the row and the company it keeps. That is the whole reason the glimpse line exists.

Build D. A is too quiet — it answers “pick from a list” with a control that hides the list. B is the most informative and the most expensive: 921px at Mozart’s six candidates against a 480px cap. C is the best teacher and the worst card. D throws away everything the other three argue about and keeps the only two facts a reader acts on — the name, and whether it is already yours — and it does it inside a control the card already has, at 315px and 422px.

What the other three were for. They are the argument D no longer makes out loud. The ranking below still orders D’s names; the default rule still decides which lane a bare press of Timeline uses; the scoring still decides which names appear at all. D shows none of it, so all of it has to be right unseen — which is why the reasoning stays on this page rather than in the card.

What I could not resolve

  • Membership has nowhere to live. Nothing in the data can record “this id is in that lane” for anything that is not literally a member row. anchors is per-lane and hard-coded (MZ_ANCHOR is a constant in layers.ts). A picker writes a membership and there is no field to write it to.
  • A lane is not enough for Daoism. Even after you pick Religion, the timeline still cannot draw a belief stream: it has no start-shaped mark, no category, no importance. Something must project a stream into an EVENTS tuple. That is a data change, not a UI one, and this prototype does not pretend otherwise.
  • Austria is not expressible. Four regions, and the only nation-shaped lane in the corpus — Czech history — exists because someone hand-wrote 38 members. “Mozart as part of Austria” needs either a fifth axis or a curated lane per country, and those are very different projects.
  • Word overlap is noisy, and I left the noise visible. Literature scores on the word wolfgang — it is matching Goethe. Read the reason on that row: this is the weakest of the three signals and the card should not hide it.
  • D shows an order it does not justify. The names are in candidate rank, added ones first, and nothing on the card says why. That is fine at the top — Music then Mozart for him, Religion then Philosophy for it, both right by feel — and soft at the tail, where Literature sits fourth for Mozart on the strength of the word wolfgang, which is Goethe. An unexplained order has to be right by feel, and this one is right where it is read and arguable where it is not.
  • The founder named Connections as one of the views; the shipped card has no Connections cell — its fixed row is Timeline, Flow, Map, Cube. D renders the shipped four rather than quietly inventing a fifth. Which of the two is wrong is his call, not mine.
  • Religion over Philosophy is 6 to 5. Nothing in the data settles where Daoism belongs, and a Sinologist might well say Philosophy. A default this thin has to be visibly reversible, which is why the notice and its Undo are part of the specimen and not an afterthought.

What would have to change in layers.ts

  1. factsOf(id) stops returning one lane. own: string | null and adopted: string[] become one ranked cands: { id, source, score, why }[]. And it needs a belief: branch reading BELIEFS.systems[].streams[], exactly as it already has a polity: and a spread: branch — that single branch is what turns never into a list for 61 streams.
  2. planReveal stops choosing. It keeps returning a plan, but for a given lane, and exposes the ranked list beside it — so the card can draw the alternatives while search keeps acting with no choice at all.
  3. A membership store beside Layers.vis / det / gvis: Layers.mem: Record<laneId, string[]>, persisted in the same tl.layers.v1 blob, and consulted by the renderer’s membership test. That is the smallest change that makes “add this to that lane” mean anything at all.
  4. RevealUndo gains mem: string[] | undefined and undoReveal restores it through the same put() delete-if-absent discipline — otherwise an undone add leaves a membership behind, which is precisely the bug that record was built to prevent.
  5. didLine() gains one case. A reveal that also writes a membership has to say both things, or Undo undoes something the reader was never told about. The specimens above say Added “Religion” and put Daoism in it.
  6. The never verdict shrinks to “there is genuinely nothing”, and the “Show in Beliefs” offer goes away with it.
  7. D only: dests() in selcard.ts stops drawing the Timeline cell shut for a belief stream. It reads planReveal(s.id).need === 'never' today and puts “there is no lane to add” on the cell — once a lane is a choice, that sentence is false, and D’s Daoism card is live precisely there.
  8. D only: the card needs the reader’s board to draw its two states. That part needs nothing new — Layers.has(laneId) already answers it — but the card has never asked, so paint() has to subscribe to the layer store the way it already subscribes to TimeStore.
